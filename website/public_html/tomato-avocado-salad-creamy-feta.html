🥑 Tomato-Avocado Salad With Creamy Feta Dressing

Ripe tomatoes, chickpeas, onion, and avocado finished with a creamy walnut-feta dressing, cilantro, and lemon. Original recipe by Ashley Lonsdale for NYT Cooking

English Español
← Back to Recipes
Prep Time10 minutes
Marinating5–10 minutes
Total Time25 minutes
Servings4

For the Salad

  • 2 pounds ripe tomatoes, cut into ½- to 1-inch pieces or wedges
  • 1 (15-ounce) can chickpeas, drained
  • 1 small sweet or white onion, halved and thinly sliced
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 ripe Hass avocados
  • Extra-virgin olive oil, for drizzling

For the Creamy Feta Dressing

  • ½ cup chopped toasted walnuts, divided
  • ½ cup chopped fresh cilantro, divided
  • 3 tablespoons fresh lemon juice
  • 3 ounces feta, crumbled, plus feta brine if available
  • 1 garlic clove, smashed and peeled
  • 1 tablespoon cold water, plus more as needed
  • 3 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

Instructions

1
In a large bowl, combine the tomatoes, chickpeas, and sliced onion with a generous pinch of salt and a light dusting of pepper. Set aside for 5–10 minutes to marinate.
2
Meanwhile, add ¼ cup walnuts and ¼ cup cilantro to a small food processor or blender, reserving the rest for garnish. Add the lemon juice, feta, garlic, and 1 tablespoon cold water or feta brine.
3
Blend until the dressing begins to turn light green, adding 1–2 teaspoons more water as needed to loosen it. Add 3 tablespoons olive oil and pulse only until emulsified and creamy. Do not overblend. Season with salt and pepper.
4
Halve and pit the avocados. Slice the flesh without cutting through the skin, then scoop the slices out in one piece and arrange them in overlapping rows on a platter or in a low, wide serving bowl. Season with salt and a drizzle of olive oil.
5
Spoon the tomato-chickpea mixture beside the avocado. Drizzle the creamy feta dressing over everything and finish with the reserved walnuts and cilantro.

💡 Cook's Tips

  • Check the walnuts: Smell them before using; they should smell neutral or nutty, never stale or rancid.
  • Toast for more flavor: Briefly toasting the walnuts improves their aroma and crunch. Let them cool before blending.
  • Use ripe tomatoes: This simple salad depends on flavorful, in-season tomatoes.
  • Pulse the olive oil: Overblending extra-virgin olive oil can introduce bitter notes.
  • No appliance: Mash the feta and finely mince the walnuts, cilantro, and garlic for a more rustic dressing.
