🥑 Shrimp and Avocado Salad With Peanut Dressing

Tender shrimp, creamy avocado, and crisp lettuce topped with a warm, crunchy peanut-sesame dressing. Original recipe by Ashley Lonsdale for NYT Cooking

English Español
← Back to Recipes
Prep Time5 minutes
Cook Time15 minutes
Total Time20 minutes
Servings4

For the Salad

  • 3–4 heads Little Gem lettuce, leaves separated, or 1 head romaine, roughly chopped
  • 2 ripe avocados, halved, pitted, and sliced lengthwise
  • Salt and freshly cracked black pepper
  • 1 pound large shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 1 tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil

For the Peanut Dressing

  • ¼ cup extra-virgin olive oil
  • 3 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • ½ cup roasted unsalted peanuts
  • 2 tablespoons white sesame seeds
  • 1 tablespoon honey
  • ¼ teaspoon fennel seeds
  • ¼ cup apple cider vinegar
  • Salt and freshly cracked black pepper, to taste

Instructions

1
Arrange the lettuce in an even layer on a large platter. Nestle the avocado slices on and around the lettuce, then season with salt and pepper.
2
Pat the shrimp dry and season with salt and pepper. Heat 1 tablespoon olive oil in a medium skillet over medium-high heat. Cook the shrimp for 3–5 minutes, turning once, until just opaque and cooked through. Transfer to a plate to cool.
3
In the same skillet, heat the remaining ¼ cup olive oil over medium heat. Add the scallions and garlic and fry, stirring frequently, for 2–3 minutes, until fragrant and the garlic is just golden.
4
Add the peanuts, sesame seeds, honey, and fennel seeds. Fry for about 1 minute, stirring occasionally, until the peanuts and sesame seeds are fragrant, toasted, and warmed through.
5
Remove the skillet from the heat and stir in the apple cider vinegar. Season the warm dressing with salt and pepper.
6
Add the cooled shrimp to the salad, spoon the warm peanut dressing over everything, and serve immediately.

💡 Cook's Tips

  • Dry the shrimp: Patting them thoroughly dry encourages browning instead of steaming.
  • Do not overcook: Remove the shrimp as soon as they turn opaque so they stay tender.
  • Watch the garlic: Fry only until lightly golden; it can become bitter quickly.
  • Add vinegar off heat: This prevents excessive splattering and preserves the dressing's bright acidity.
  • Protein alternatives: Salmon, chicken, or steak can replace the shrimp while keeping the same salad and dressing.
