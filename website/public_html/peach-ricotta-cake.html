🍑 Peach Ricotta Cake

A moist, lemon-scented ricotta cake with chopped peaches folded into the batter and beautiful peach wedges baked on top. Original recipe by Yossy Arefi for NYT Cooking

English Español
← Back to Recipes
Prep Time5 minutes
Cook & Cool Time1 hour 10 minutes
Total Time1 hour 15 minutes
YieldOne 9-inch cake; 8–10 servings

Ingredients

  • ½ cup (113 g) unsalted butter, softened, plus more for the pan
  • 2 large peaches, about ¾ pound (340 g) total
  • 1 cup (200 g) granulated sugar, plus 2 teaspoons for the topping
  • 1 large lemon
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 cup (230 g) whole-milk ricotta
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • ¾ teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1½ cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1½ teaspoons baking powder
  • ½ teaspoon baking soda

Instructions

1
Position a rack in the center of the oven and heat to 350°F (175°C). Butter a 9-inch round cake pan and line the bottom with parchment paper.
2
Cut the peaches into ½-inch-thick slices. Chop about one-quarter of the slices into ½-inch pieces and set them aside separately.
3
Put 1 cup sugar in a large bowl or stand-mixer bowl. Finely zest the lemon directly over the sugar, then rub the zest into the sugar with your fingertips to release the oils.
4
Add the softened butter and beat on medium-high until light and fluffy, about 3 minutes. Add the eggs one at a time, mixing for 30 seconds after each addition.
5
Add the ricotta, vanilla, and salt. Mix until smooth and emulsified, about 30 seconds.
6
Add the flour, baking powder, and baking soda. Fold with a flexible spatula until smooth and well combined, scraping the bottom and sides of the bowl. Fold in the chopped peaches.
7
Spread the batter in the prepared pan. Tap the pan on the counter a few times to release large air bubbles. Arrange the remaining peach slices in a single layer on top and sprinkle with the remaining 2 teaspoons sugar.
8
Bake for 50–60 minutes, until puffed and golden and a tester inserted in the center comes out clean.
9
Cool in the pan on a wire rack for about 15 minutes. Invert onto a plate, remove the parchment, then invert again onto the rack so the peach side faces up. Cool completely before slicing.

💡 Cook's Tips

  • Use ripe but firm peaches: They should be flavorful while still holding their shape during slicing and baking.
  • Stone-fruit substitutes: Use the same weight of nectarines, plums, or another fresh stone fruit.
  • Frozen peaches: A 12-ounce package works; thaw completely and pat the slices dry before adding them.
  • Rub the zest into the sugar: This releases the lemon oils and distributes their aroma throughout the cake.
  • Storage: Wrap tightly and keep at room temperature or in the refrigerator for up to 2 days.
