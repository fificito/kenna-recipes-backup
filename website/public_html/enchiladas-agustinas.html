Main Course
Mexican
Puebla
A classic Pueblan dish from chile en nogada season: tortillas filled with the same sweet-savory picadillo as chiles en nogada, then smothered in mole poblano and garnished with pomegranate seeds, sesame, and parsley. A wonderful option for those who prefer not to eat the battered chile version — same spirit, new form.

Ingredients

  • 300 g pork leg, diced small (not ground)
  • ½ white onion
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 2 tomatoes
  • 1 tablespoon powdered chicken bouillon (consomé)
  • 1 plantain, diced
  • 2 Zacatlán apples, peeled and diced
  • 5 pear apples (peras lecheras), peeled and diced
  • 2 peaches, peeled and diced
  • ½ cup raisins
  • 1 cup blanched almonds
  • Sugar, to taste
  • ½ kg corn tortillas
  • Oil, for frying
  • 2 cups mole poblano
  • Pomegranate seeds, sesame seeds, and parsley, for garnish

Instructions

1Cook the Pork

In a pot, cook the pork with a bit of oil, diced onion, and garlic over medium heat until the meat releases its juices and begins to brown.

2Cook the Fruit

In a separate large pot, cook the tomatoes with the bouillon until they release their juices. Add the fruit (plantain, apples, pears, peaches), almonds, raisins, and a little sugar to taste. Cook until the fruit softens and the flavors meld.

3Combine and Season

Mix the cooked fruit with the pork. Taste and adjust seasoning as needed. Set aside.

4Fry the Tortillas and Fill

Heat enough oil in a skillet. Quickly fry each tortilla for a few seconds per side until softened and slightly golden (not crispy). Transfer to a plate and fill each tortilla with the pork and fruit mixture. Roll or fold and place on a serving dish.

5Cover with Mole

Ladle the mole poblano generously over the filled tortillas, covering them completely.

6Garnish and Serve

Finish with a generous sprinkle of pomegranate seeds, sesame seeds, and fresh parsley. Serve immediately.

📝 Chef's Tips

  • Seasonal dish: This is a classic of Puebla's chile en nogada season (August–September). The picadillo filling is the same as for chiles en nogada — make it for both and use leftovers here.
  • Mole poblano: Use homemade mole poblano for best results. The recipe author links to her own mole recipe on Pola Cocina.
  • Fruit swaps: Peras lecheras (a small, sweet Mexican pear variety) can be replaced with regular Bartlett or Anjou pears. Zacatlán apples are a sweet Mexican apple — substitute with Gala or Golden Delicious.
  • Quick fry the tortillas: Don't skip this step — a brief fry in oil makes the tortillas pliable and gives them extra flavor. They should be soft, not crispy.
  • Make ahead: The picadillo filling can be made a day ahead and refrigerated. Assemble and sauce just before serving.
  • No batter, no chile: Perfect for guests who don't eat fried foods or can't handle spicy chiles, while still capturing the flavors of the season.
