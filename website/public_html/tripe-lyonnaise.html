🥘 Tripe Lyonnaise

Tender tripe simmered with bacon, tomato and warm spices, then fried until golden and finished with slowly caramelized onions, Chardonnay vinegar and parsley. Original recipe by Greg

Ingredients

  • 1 kg tripe
  • 4 brown onions
  • 3 bay leaves
  • 1 can tomatoes
  • 1 litre chicken stock
  • 4 rashers bacon
  • 8 garlic cloves
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1 teaspoon cloves
  • Forum Chardonnay vinegar, a good splash
  • 1 knob butter, or to taste
  • 1 bunch parsley, chopped
  • Plain flour, for coating
  • Neutral oil, for sautéing and shallow-frying

Instructions

1
In a saucepan over medium heat, sauté the bacon with 5 garlic cloves and 1 roughly chopped onion for about 10 minutes, until the bacon and onion gain some color.
2
Deglaze with the chicken stock. Add the bay leaves, tomatoes, cloves and cinnamon stick, then bring to a boil.
3
Wash the tripe thoroughly and add it to the saucepan. Lower the heat and simmer for 2–3 hours. Remove the tripe from the stock and allow it to cool.
4
Finely slice the remaining 3 onions. Sauté them gently with the remaining 3 whole garlic cloves for about 45 minutes, until the onions are soft, stringy and caramelized. Remove and discard the garlic.
5
Slice the cooled tripe into strips and coat them lightly with flour, shaking off the excess. Heat oil until very hot and quickly shallow-fry the tripe until golden.
6
Add the butter and caramelized onions to the tripe and stir together. Finish with a generous splash of Chardonnay vinegar and the chopped parsley. Toss well and serve immediately.

💡 Cook's Tips

  • Use cleaned tripe: Ask the butcher for properly cleaned tripe and rinse it thoroughly before cooking.
  • Cook until tender: The 2–3 hour simmer is a guide; the tripe should be tender before it is cooled and fried.
  • Caramelize slowly: Keep the heat gentle so the onions soften and sweeten without scorching.
  • Fry in batches: Avoid crowding the pan so the floured tripe browns instead of steaming.
  • Balance at the end: The vinegar supplies the sour counterpoint to the sweet onions; add it gradually and taste.
